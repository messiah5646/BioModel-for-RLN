## Gate D — FAILED. The schedule-dependence claim is not novel.

**The bet:** that §5.4's finding — pulse schedule matters at fixed total light dose, worth
up to ~9× — was a novel contribution.

**It is not.** Pulse-width-modulated optogenetics is an established technique with over a
decade of history:

- **Olson et al.-lineage work, 2013** — "Programming Microbes Using Pulse Width Modulation
  of Optical Signals." PWM as a deliberate optogenetic control strategy.
- **IEEE, 2016** — "Pulse-Width Modulation of Optogenetic Photo-Stimulation Intensity for
  Application to Full-Implantable Light Sources." PWM used explicitly to decouple
  delivered intensity from hardware output.
- **"Dynamic Multiplexed Control and Modeling of Optogenetic Systems" (bioRxiv 2023,
  2023.12.19.572411)** — the closest prior art. It screens intensity × period × duty cycle,
  shows each shapes the response, and **attributes the differences to "the response
  kinetics (activation and reversion time)" of the photoswitches**. Uses
  CRY2(L348F)/CIB1 and eMagAF/eMagBF — the same switch families in our toolbox.
- A *Methods in Enzymology* chapter, "Pulsatile illumination for photobiology and
  optogenetics," exists as a protocols reference.

**Retraction.** `positioning.md` §3D listed the NC3 law as "the most transferable"
contribution and flagged it as killed by exactly this literature. It is killed. The
underlying phenomenon — schedule-dependence arising from photoswitch on/off kinetics — is
known to the optogenetics field and was named as such in 2023.

### What survives, stated narrowly

1. **The NC3 compliance test as an audit protocol for world models.** Using
   schedule-at-fixed-dose as a *discriminator between an action in the transition operator
   and an action on an output head* is a claim about model architecture, not biology. No
   evidence found that anyone has used it this way. This is a **framing and testing**
   contribution, not a discovery. Weaker, but still ours, and it is the part that matters
   for §5.2's purpose.
2. **A narrow mechanistic gap.** The 2023 paper's predictor is a feed-forward neural
   network mapping intensity/duty/period directly to induction; it explicitly **does not
   use on/off rate constants as inputs** and does not fit a kinetic model. Our §5.2 result
   is generated by explicit `k_on`/`k_off` kinetics. So "predict schedule response
   mechanistically from measured photoswitch rate constants" may still be open — **but it
   is a small gap, and the 2023 paper limited periods to 4 hours, so our longer-period
   regime is partly untested rather than unclaimed.**
3. **The 0.00% stateless result.** Almost certainly a restatement of standard periodic
   averaging. **Do not claim it.** Cite averaging theory instead.

**Net: D drops from "most transferable contribution" to "a test protocol we use
internally." It should not anchor a paper.**

## Gate A — UNRESOLVED, blocked.

The threat to contribution A is "Systematic Review of Trial Design and End Points in Lupus
Nephritis" (*Kidney Int Rep* 2026, PMID 42006208, PMC13090550). **Could not be read.** PMC
and PubMed both served reCAPTCHA; the publisher fulltext and a trade summary both returned
client errors. This is round 37 §9's known intermittent PMC block, now hitting a

**Reasoning in the absence of the text, flagged as reasoning, not evidence:** a trial-design
review answers *which endpoints trials use and how much they vary.* Contribution A answers
*which parameters are estimable from a given measurement schedule.* These are different
questions, and the second is not standard practice in the trial literature. The review's
own framing — that endpoints need standardising to allow cross-study comparison —
**strengthens** A's motivation rather than pre-empting it.

But that is an argument, not a check. **A is not cleared.** It needs a browser session with
institutional access, the same unresolved blocker as round 37's PMC13337175.

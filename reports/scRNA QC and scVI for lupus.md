# Filter gently, integrate modestly, classify donors simply

Current best practice for single-cell QC is permissive and per sample, and it flags cells rather than deleting them. Cells are flagged at 5 MADs on log counts, log genes and top-20-gene share, and at 3 MADs (upper side only) on mito fraction, with a tissue-aware mito ceiling. Ambient RNA correction and scDblFinder run separately for each capture. Stress and cell-cycle programmes are scored, not regressed. scVI is the right default for integrating weak batch effects within a cohort. sysVI is the choice for strong "system" shifts such as single-cell versus single-nucleus data, and scANVI/scArches covers reference mapping. None of these has been shown to beat simple representations at the task LUMEN cares about most: classifying donors or donor-timepoints. A 2026 benchmark across 11 cohorts and 697 individuals ranked CLR cell-type composition first and pseudobulk third for patient stratification, ahead of MrVI and scPoli. Foundation-model embeddings routinely carry more batch information than the data they were computed from. The project's own round 98 fits this pattern. Three QC numbers alone gave AUC 0.66 for pre versus post B-cell depletion, and matching cells on QC cut PCA's lead over a 6-gene IFN score from 0.14 to 0.035. So the burden of proof sits with the deep model. For the open lupus data, the binding constraints are design, not method. In Perez 2022, flare is nested inside one processing cohort. In the CAR-T and A-319 trials, every timepoint is its own library. Open lupus nephritis (LN) kidney single-cell data amount to one biopsy. The protocol at the end therefore tests at donor level, pits every embedding against QC-only, composition, pseudobulk and IFN-score baselines, and treats the kidney arm as descriptive only.

## Permissive per-sample MAD flags beat any fixed threshold

The scverse "Single-cell best practices" book flags outliers at **5 MAD on `log1p_total_counts`, `log1p_n_genes_by_counts` and `pct_counts_in_top_20_genes`**. It scores mito as a logit fraction and flags it at **3 MAD, upper side only, and only if the cell is also above 8% mito**. The book warns that the 8% floor "is not universal", calls the scheme "relatively permissive", and in its example removes about 5.5% of cells ([sc-best-practices QC](https://www.sc-best-practices.org/preprocessing-visualization/quality-control/)). Heumos et al. 2023 make the per-sample rule explicit: thresholds "can vary substantially between samples", metrics should be considered jointly, and filtering should start permissive and tighten only if needed ([Heumos 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00586-w.pdf)). Fixed mito caps do measurable damage. A 10% cap removed **41% of cells** from a human blood-derived 10x dataset, against 18.5% with the adaptive miQC model ([miQC](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.03.433798v1.full-text)). Kidney is the extreme case. KPMP kept 10x kidney cells with mito **below 50%** and 500–5,000 genes, and it **flagged and kept** injured or "altered" tubule states instead of filtering them ([Lake 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05769-3.pdf)). AMP Phase 1 used a 25% mito cap for kidney cells and 8% for 10x blood ([Arazi 2019](https://hacohenlab.mgh.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Arazi-et-al-Nat-Immun-2019.pdf)). No published head-to-head compares per-sample with pooled MAD thresholds. The per-sample rule rests on expert consensus, not measurement.

Ambient RNA is the second front, and the evidence there is better than for thresholds. On mouse kidney with genotype ground truth, background made up roughly **3–35% of UMIs per cell**. CellBender was most accurate, SoupX underestimated background in whole-cell data, and the proximal-tubule marker Slc34a1 fell from about 60% of non-PT cells to 7% with CellBender versus 54% with SoupX ([Janssen 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s13059-023-02978-x)). The same study found correction changed under 1% of cell labels. Its advice is to always correct before marker or DE work, but to correct before clustering only when background is high. A January 2026 preprint testing 7 tools on 49 samples ranks **CellBender first, DecontX (full) second and SoupX (reduced) third**. It also notes that scAR over-removes endogenous signal and that only SoupX's reduced mode works when only filtered matrices exist ([Cargnelli 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.13.699237.full.pdf)). That last point matters here, because every open lupus PBMC deposit described below ships filtered matrices. Two lupus-specific soups are untested inferences but worth measuring: haemoglobin and platelet transcripts, and, in active SLE with plasmablast expansion, immunoglobulin transcripts that could leak into T, NK and myeloid cells.

For doublets, scDblFinder is the current recommendation. It must run per capture and should flag cells rather than delete them ([sc-best-practices QC](https://www.sc-best-practices.org/preprocessing-visualization/quality-control/)). Its lead in the Xi & Li datasets is real but flattered by tuning on those same datasets. At its automatic threshold it recovers about 87% of heterotypic doublets with an FDR near 17% ([Germain 2022](https://f1000research.com/articles/10-979/v2)). The original benchmark's best mean AUPRC was only **0.537** ([Xi & Li 2021](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6gr648np/qt6gr648np_noSplash_32134c0e501d9fdf306702f2a4dcc052.pdf)), so no detector is close to clean. Dissociation and thaw stress (FOS, JUN, HSPA1A) and cell cycle follow the same logic: score them, inspect them, and leave them in the data. AMP found that regressing plate, UMI and mito "had a negligible effect" ([Arazi 2019](https://hacohenlab.mgh.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Arazi-et-al-Nat-Immun-2019.pdf)). Heumos notes that cycling status is often the biology itself ([Heumos 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00586-w.pdf)), and in lupus that includes plasmablasts and proliferating T cells.

The over-filtering risks are specific to lupus. Neutrophils and low-density granulocytes carry only a few hundred genes, and default cell calling drops them. In 10x's own example, default calling found 1,886 cells where about 8,000 were expected ([10x neutrophil tutorial](https://www.10xgenomics.com/support/software/cell-ranger/tutorials/cr-tutorial-neutrophils)). Plasmablasts, which are dominated by Ig transcripts, plausibly exceed the upper 5-MAD bound on counts and on top-20-gene share. No source quantifies this, but plasmablast burden is a lupus activity readout the project has already used (round 97). So the QC step must check whether flags concentrate in MZB1+/JCHAIN+ cells before anything is removed.

## scVI fits weak batch effects, sysVI strong ones, and neither fixes confounding

The scVI defaults (n_latent=10, n_layers=1, n_hidden=128, ZINB, gene-level dispersion) are a starting point. A model meant to serve as a scArches reference should use **n_layers=2, dropout 0.2, layer norm, no batch norm, encode_covariates=True, and about 2,000 batch-aware HVGs chosen on the reference** ([scvi-tools reference mapping tutorial](https://docs.scvi-tools.org/en/1.0.3/tutorials/notebooks/scrna/scarches_scvi_tools.html)). Early stopping is off by default and has to be switched on ([SCVI API](https://docs.scvi-tools.org/en/1.3.3/api/reference/scvi.model.SCVI.html)). The covariate slots are explicitly "nuisance factors, which the model tries to remove from the latent space", and they "should not be used for biologically-relevant factors" ([SCVI API](https://docs.scvi-tools.org/en/1.3.3/api/reference/scvi.model.SCVI.html)). A scverse developer warns that "adding continuous covariates can make the latent space significantly worse" ([scverse discourse](https://discourse.scverse.org/t/nested-batch-effects-with-scvi/1910)). Library size is already a model offset, so passing n_counts as a covariate is redundant. Passing % mito would strip real proximal-tubule and activation biology. Neither should go in by default.

sysVI (Hrovatin et al., BMC Genomics 2025) adds a VampPrior and a cycle-consistency loss. It gave significantly stronger batch correction than a cVAE baseline across cross-species, organoid-versus-tissue and sc-versus-sn tasks, with comparable biological preservation and about twice scVI's runtime ([Hrovatin 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12864-025-12126-3)). The scvi-tools guide reserves it for substantial effects and keeps scVI for weak ones, such as many subjects from one lab, where scVI preserves "slightly higher" biology ([sysVI guide](https://docs.scvi-tools.org/en/stable/user_guide/models/sysvi.html)). The same paper shows two ways that aggressive correction destroys biology. Raising the KL weight collapses latent dimensions, and adversarial alignment merges unrelated cell types. The scIB-E preprint adds that metrics computed at the label level miss the erosion of variation *within* cell types, and on one atlas plain scVI scored best at preserving that variation ([scIB-E 2024](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.09.627450.full.pdf)). For lupus this is decisive. Disease and therapy effects live inside cell types (IFN-high monocytes, for example), so a high scIB score says nothing about whether the disease signal survived integration.

Reference mapping works as advertised when the reference is built for it. The steps are `prepare_query_anndata` to align genes, training only the query parameters with weight_decay=0, and transferring labels with scANVI `predict()`, which reached about 97% accuracy in the tutorial ([reference mapping tutorial](https://docs.scvi-tools.org/en/1.0.3/tutorials/notebooks/scrna/scarches_scvi_tools.html)). No pretrained scvi-tools model was found for Perez 2022 or KPMP, so LUMEN would have to train its own reference. For DE, pseudobulk on raw counts per donor × cell type is the validated standard. In 18 ground-truth datasets, the six best of 14 DE methods were all pseudobulk, and RNAscope confirmed 5/6 pseudobulk hits against 3/13 Wilcoxon hits ([Squair 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25960-2)). scVI's `transform_batch` decodes cells counterfactually along nuisance axes only. It cannot simulate treatment, which matters for a project whose stated aim is in-silico trials.

## Deep latents have not beaten simple baselines at donor-level classification

The independent evidence from 2023–2026 points one way. Zero-shot Geneformer and scGPT lost to HVG selection, Harmony and scVI at cell-type clustering, and "HVG beats both foundation models on all metrics". Geneformer's embedding had a *higher* share of variance explained by batch than the input data ([Kedzierska 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12007350)). A December 2025 preprint tested eight foundation models. All but one underperformed Harmony and scVI, some fell below PCA, and linear probes recovered batch more easily from foundation embeddings than from PCA ([bioRxiv 2025.12.19.695371](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.19.695371v1.full)). Fine-tuned scBERT and scGPT mostly failed to beat logistic regression, except under distribution shift ([Boiarsky 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.10.19.563100.full.pdf)). For perturbation prediction, "none outperformed the baselines" ([Ahlmann-Eltze 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12328236/)), and a train-mean baseline beat scGPT and scFoundation on all four datasets tested ([Csendes 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12016270)). Rounds 92 and 98, where Geneformer lost to PCA, match this record. They are not an anomaly.

scVI should be judged separately from foundation models. Its integration advantage is real on complex, nested-batch atlases. Even there it depends on the dataset: one benchmark found plain HVG selection scored best on batch integration on all five datasets ([Kedzierska 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12007350)). For *sample-level* labels, the best evidence favours simple representations. Halter et al. (bioRxiv 2026; 11 cohorts, 697 individuals, autoimmune included) ranked **ECODA (CLR composition) first, then GloProp and pseudobulk, then GloScope, MOFA+, MrVI and scPoli**. The baselines ran in seconds, while MrVI, scPoli and GloScope needed hours of GPU time and downsampling ([Halter 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.27.714811v1.full)). That benchmark is unsupervised and includes no lupus cohort, so it does not settle supervised donor-level AUC. The only large learned-model win, PaSCient, used about 5,000 samples and study-level splits. Even its authors note that "a simple pseudo-bulk approach outperforms more complicated foundation models in this task" ([PaSCient](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.18.624166v2.full.pdf)). LUMEN's cohorts have 6 to 160 donors, so that result does not transfer. A small donor-aware IBD study found that CLR composition (AUROC 0.956), pseudobulk XGBoost (1.000, likely overfit) and scVI features (0.931) did not differ significantly. Its scVI model was also trained on all donors before cross-validation ([Muhire 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.03281)).

MrVI is the most principled deep model for cohort designs ([MrVI guide](https://docs.scvi-tools.org/en/stable/user_guide/models/mrvi.html)). It separates a sample-invariant latent from a sample-aware one and gives cell-resolved sample-distance matrices and counterfactual DE. The published version, however, handles one target and one nuisance covariate and does not model repeated measures ([Boyeau preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.10.04.510898.full.pdf)), and it ranked below composition and pseudobulk in Halter 2026. Its proper role in LUMEN is exploratory: finding *which* cell states separate donors, not serving as a classifier. Leakage is the final reason to distrust impressive single-cell AUCs. Random cell-level splits let models "memorise relatives", and in one patient cohort the inflation was large enough to "overturn a clinical conclusion" ([Cang & Sun 2026](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.64898/2026.09.09.750484)). Round 98 used leave-one-donor-out but still scored cells. The stricter test makes one prediction per donor-timepoint.

## QC covariates leak into embeddings non-linearly, and only design breaks the confound

Round 98 is a clean case study. It used 2,200 monocytes from 11 SLE donors before and 3 months after A-319 (CELLxGENE dataset 3979a248). Depth, genes detected and % mito alone gave **AUC 0.66**, against 0.68 for the IFN score, 0.82 for PCA-50 and 0.77 for Geneformer. Linear residualisation on the three QC numbers left PCA's edge intact. QC matching, under which QC alone fell to 0.49, cut it to 0.035 (project doc `claude/round-98-the-IFN-score-survives-QC-adjustment-the-embedding-edge-mostly-does-not.md`). The interpretation follows: technical structure is spread non-linearly across 50 latent dimensions, which a linear regression on three scalars cannot remove. The external literature points the same way. Foundation embeddings amplify batch ([Kedzierska 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12007350)). Per-batch mean-centring removes it only partly ([bioRxiv 2025.12.19.695371](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.19.695371v1.full)). High-dropout clusters get misclassified on technical grounds ([Souza & Mehta 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16696)). Pseudobulk is the representation most exposed to batch: in one cohort its batch ANOSIM (**0.706**) exceeded its biological ANOSIM (0.322), while CLR composition showed 0.041 versus 0.640 ([Halter 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.27.714811v1.full)).

The open lupus trials share the worst possible structure: each donor-timepoint is its own 10x library. In that design, library, processing day, thaw quality and treatment are the same variable. Whatever separates pre from post could be any of them. Setting `batch_key` to sample deletes the treatment signal. Setting it to donor keeps the signal but leaves the library confound in place. MrVI cannot resolve this either. Declaring batch as nuisance removes the effect, and omitting it leaves the confound. Composition is the most robust to the confound, but it inherits whatever the QC thresholds remove, so its stability across QC settings has to be reported. The only clean fix is prospective: hashtag or genotype multiplexing that puts pre and post cells into one library. The retrospective substitute is a control panel. It consists of a QC-only classifier, the increment of each embedding over QC alone, re-evaluation on QC-matched cells, a batch probe, a donor-label permutation null, and donor-level aggregation. Matching is the sharper diagnostic and residualisation the more conservative one, so both are reported.

Four consequences follow. First, Perez has only two clean contrasts. Healthy versus managed SLE is clean within cohort 4 (52 managed, 44 controls, balanced on ancestry). Flare versus managed or control is clean only within cohort 3, which holds 19 flare, 4 managed and 18 controls, plus 10 paired flare-to-treated donors. Any whole-atlas flare classifier partly learns cohort. The authors already regressed nUMI and mito and ran ComBat on their processed object ([Perez 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297655/)), so LUMEN should start from `raw.X`. Second, none of the open PBMC deposits includes raw droplet matrices. Ambient correction is therefore limited to SoupX in reduced mode, or to measuring the ambient load and declaring it ([Cargnelli 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.13.699237.full.pdf)). Third, the trial datasets are small paired designs: 7, 11 and 4 patients with repeated samples. With 11 pairs, an exact sign-flip test has 2,048 permutations, so the smallest attainable p-value is about 0.0005. Only large effects are detectable, and a deep model would need to beat the best baseline by a wide margin to show anything. Fourth, LN kidney analysis on open data cannot be inferential. One biopsy supports a descriptive mapping onto a KPMP-trained reference with uncertainty flags, and nothing more. The kidney classification question needs an AMP/ARK access application, which falls outside the open-only constraint ([AMP Phase 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13060338/)). Pooling 3′ v2 (Perez, hg19) with 5′ v2 (trials, GRCh38) requires harmonising genes on Ensembl IDs and treating chemistry as a system-level batch.

## A pre-registrable protocol for LUMEN

The protocol tests one hypothesis family: **H1, a learned latent space (scVI, sysVI where justified, Geneformer as negative control) gives a donor-level classification gain over the best simple baseline that survives QC control**. The null is the pattern seen in round 98, where the gain vanishes after QC matching. The tables below fix the settings in advance. Every threshold is a pre-registered default, and any change after unblinding is reported as a deviation.

**Pre-registered decision rules and controls.** The primary metric is donor-level AUC, or paired accuracy for the paired contrasts, with a donor-bootstrap 95% CI. H1 holds for a contrast only if all four conditions below are met. If any one fails, the result is reported as "no demonstrated gain", which is the outcome the literature predicts.

1. D1 beats the best of B1–B4 by at least **0.05 AUC**, and the paired-bootstrap CI excludes 0.
2. D1 beats B0 by at least the same margin, and D1+B0 beats B0 alone.
3. The gain survives re-running on QC-matched cells, with nearest-neighbour matching on B0 metrics within cell type. The mandatory check is that B0 drops to about 0.5 after matching.
4. A batch probe trained on the embedding does not predict library or cohort better than it predicts the label.

The rules also cover leakage, robustness and reporting. Every unsupervised model is fitted inside the fold, and any transductive fit is labelled as such. Results are reported under the default and a strict QC setting (3 MAD on all metrics), because composition shifts with filtering. Linear QC residualisation is reported alongside matching. Within-cell-type DE runs as pseudobulk (edgeR or limma-voom) on raw counts, with donor pairing in the design. One analytic seed and one hyperparameter set are frozen before results are seen. Each round's prereg doc records the hash of the QC and model config.

## Conclusion

The question has shifted from "which deep model" to "which baseline would a deep model have to beat, under which design". In the open lupus data, the strongest competitor is the QC table: it predicts timepoint about as well as biology does, because every timepoint is its own library. This has a direct consequence for LUMEN's simulation goal. A latent space that cannot beat composition and an IFN score at donor level under QC matching gives no basis for counterfactual "trial fast-forwarding". scVI's counterfactuals move only nuisance axes in any case. The honest near-term use of scVI is integration and annotation infrastructure, a shared, QC-transparent cell-state map across Perez and the three trials. The donor-level signal should be carried by interpretable features: composition, pseudobulk programmes and IFN and plasmablast scores. The one result that would change this is a deep latent that passes all four H1 conditions on C1 to C4.

Two gaps decide what comes next. Kidney is a blind spot under the open-only rule, with one LN biopsy. Any claim about the kidney compartment is a mapping exercise until AMP Phase 2 access is obtained or a new open LN biopsy series appears. And no retrospective analysis can separate library from timepoint as cleanly as a multiplexed design. If LUMEN ever commissions or partners on data generation, hashtagging pre and post samples into shared libraries is worth more than any choice of model.
